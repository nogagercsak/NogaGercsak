AMP Reflection

Aug 14, 2026

I spent this past summer in NYC at Jane Street’s Academy of Math and Programming. It’s my first day back home, and although I feel safe and certainly comfortable back in my bed, my heart feels heavy. So many memories, friendships, and familiar faces disappeared in a blink of an eye. Two memories come to my mind.

First, watching the sunset on the Staten Island ferry and thinking how grateful I was to be in the present moment. I was with friends that I felt like I could be myself around. I could smell the salt water. Nothing else mattered in the moment, just being able to enjoy the experience was all the peace I needed.

Second, late night conversations with friends at the dorms. Topics ranged from random shit we did in high school, our thoughts in our classes, who we wanted to be in the future, etc. But the feelings were always the same during those conversations: belonging, gratitude, ease.

I’m getting a lot of déjà vu from my sadness when NYU GSTEM ended last summer. That same loom of emptiness is sitting with me. Last summer, I think I tried to focus more on how this emptiness can be filled with the memories and lessons from our experiences. And they will forever shape us. This summer, I am focusing more on understanding the mechanics behind these experiences.

I want to bridge this idea of the experiences with what I learned from Po Shen Loh’s talk at AMP. He argued that humans are a combination of hardware, our physical body, and our software, our culture, experiences, relationships, etc. Every single human pretty much has the same hardware, it’s our software that makes us our own individuals.

At AMP, everyone came in with their unique software. We all led different lives and feelings, all stemming from our lived experiences, our families, our hometowns, etc. And yet in that summer, all our paths intersected in New York City. I think the contradiction is how I felt more connected than ever with all these people. Of course we still had our differences, but within those differences I found admiration and love; I learned so much about others not only through skills for solving combinatorics and number theory problems but also what it means to find community and friendship. How is that possible? How are we able to find so much love with people from different corners of the world?

I think it’s made me realize we aren’t as different as I thought we were. It’s natural to initially feel distant and isolated from those who might seem different from us superficially (via traits like culture, personality, etc.), but time and kindness will transform these strangers into friends we love. Moreover, these differences are what make me love my friends even more. I want to know more about your unique life experiences and thoughts—just as much as I want to yap about our similarities.

I learned what it means to lean on my friends, how to have a fun time while also knowing there were people who supported me. If anything, this summer I learned how curiosity, kindness, and small leaps of faith are essential to fulfilling relationships.

Hmmm lastly, I am thinking a lot about how I am able to find these relationships with others. Maybe the key is to be vulnerable and yourself. When I act like my true self (even if it’s embarrassing or cringe), it makes me feel more accepted than anything. I guess another lesson was learning to not sacrifice your true self for acceptance from others, because otherwise it’s not real.

alright here’s a jumbled list of thoughts lol:

classes

  • Number Theory: When solving problems, be clear on your approach and organize your thoughts. Look for patterns.
  • Combinatorics: Sometimes we can’t follow our heuristics for solving problems, you should keep your eyes peeled for when things feel “slippery”
  • Computer Science: Working with others can allow debugging to feel like a task that can be conquered
  • Study hall: Ask questions and always be open minded to ideas you wouldn’t have thought of otherwise. Also be confident in your ability.

nyc

  • Ride bikes. Some of the most freeing moments were when I was riding the bike on the Hudson River with friends. the wind in your hair. the sun shining. the view of the river. it feels so magical
  • try new cuisines with friends. Don’t be afraid to try new flavors or cuisines. also SHARE!!!
  • take the staten island ferry (even if it’s thunderstorming right before and you think you might sink (the sunset will be worth it))
  • take a random train and get lost. Ask people for directions. Use context clues on how to get around. Spontaneity makes the best memories.
  • appreciate the art nyc has to offer—i went to the MoMA!
  • sneak into the firework show viewing at dumbo by generating fake qr codes :P

friendship

  • sacrifice the sleep for the memories
  • be loud, laugh a lot, say stupid things
  • the right people will be there for you when you need it
  • honesty can be scary but it’s necessary
  • friendship is more than a good hang out, it’s reliability, patience, and strength

Maybe I will update this later with more details, but I think overall: difficult goodbyes are a blessing. hold the memories and friendships close to your heart. they are all we have.
